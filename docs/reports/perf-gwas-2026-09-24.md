# What an association study costs, measured for the first time

24 September 2026. The gwas module merged into `main` with the two plans
`docs/plans/gwas-linear.md` and `docs/plans/gwas-logistic.md`, and nothing
had ever timed it: no benchmark, no profile, and no measurement of popnei
against the two numbers that "Speed" of `docs/specs/gwas.md` states. This
review measures it, says where the time goes, and reports four changes that
were made and kept, each of which leaves every number of every study exactly
as it was.

The study of 100000 variants of 1000 individuals now takes 0.120 s where it
took 0.365 s for a continuous trait, and 0.579 s where it took 5.020 s for a
binomial one. What is left for the owner to decide is at the end of section
2, and the largest of those decisions is whether the reader that this review
built for the study, which reads one block while the study tests the block
before it, is taken up by the kinship and the principal components as well.

## The words this document uses

An **association study** gives, for each variant of a dataset, the effect of
one more copy of a non major allele on a trait of the individuals, how
uncertain that effect is, and the p-value of the test that the effect is 0.
The four models it can fit, the two tests, and which test each model
defaults to are those of `docs/specs/gwas.md`: `lm` and `lmm`, a continuous
trait without and with a kinship, and `glm` and `glmm`, a binomial one
without and with a kinship. The **Wald test** fits the model again with the
variant in it, so it costs a fit per variant, and the **score test** costs
no fit; a binomial trait with no kinship defaults to the Wald test, and that
is the pass this review found the most in.

A **block** is the run of variants a reader gives at a time, 5000 variants
of 1000 individuals in every measurement here, so a pass over the panel is
20 blocks. The **dosages** of a block are one number per variant per tested
individual, how many alleles of that individual's genotype are not the major
one; the module computes them for a whole block and then **tests the
block**, which for every model but one is a product of the block with
something the null model holds. The exception is the Wald test of a binomial
trait, which fits one logistic regression per variant.

**rayon** is the library that runs work on several threads, and popnei used
it here for the dosages of a block and for nothing else. The **read ahead
thread** that section 3 of `docs/architecture.md` asks for, one block ahead
of the consumer, did not exist in any pass of popnei until this review;
`docs/reports/perf-kinship-2026-09-23.md` and
`docs/reports/perf-io-2026-09-22.md` each left it open as a finding nobody
had built.

A **sampling profile** stops a process a thousand times a second and records
where it is. The **self time** of a function is how many of those stops were
inside that function and not inside something it called. One sample is taken
per thread per interval, so a share of the samples of one thread is a share
of that thread's time, and shares added across threads are not shares of the
wall time; the rankings below are of one thread each and say which. The
**phase clock** is what this review added instead for the three parts of one
turn of the pass, behind a cargo feature that no build popnei ships turns
on: a profile cannot separate the reading of a block from the testing of it
while the two run on one thread.

The programs popnei is measured against. **plink2** is the standard tool of
the field and `--glm` is its command for the two models with no kinship.
**GMMAT** is the R package for the two with one, and the number "Speed" of
the spec takes from it is 1.6 s. The **panel** of every measurement here is
`/Users/jose/devel/popnei-bench/bigcalled.vars`, 100000 variants x 1000
individuals with every genotype called, popnei's own vars file of 77.8 MB;
the same genotypes are in plink2's own `.pgen` of 22.3 MB and in a VCF of
403 MB. The **machine** is the owner's Apple M5 Pro, 18 cores, 64 GB, macOS
27, native `aarch64`, and every wall time is the best of 5 runs after one
run that is not timed.

## 2. The verdict: apply, and four changes are on the branch

Four experiments were run, one at a time, and all four were kept. They are
on the branch `perf/gwas` in six commits and **nothing is merged into
`main`**. Every one of them was gated on an **identity check**: the effect,
its standard error and the p-value of all 100000 variants, written at
seventeen digits, come out **byte for byte** what they were before the
change, at one thread and at eighteen. A change made for speed that moves a
number is the owner's and not a reviewer's, so an experiment that failed
that check would have been reported and not kept; none failed it.

| model and test | before, 18 cores | after, 18 cores | before, 1 core | after, 1 core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lm` | 0.365 s | **0.120 s** | 0.434 s | 0.322 s |
| `glm`, score test | 0.293 s | **0.118 s** | 0.373 s | 0.303 s |
| `glm`, Wald test | 5.020 s | **0.579 s** | 4.965 s | 5.155 s |
| `lmm`, score test | 0.700 s | **0.549 s** | 0.794 s | 0.671 s |
| `lmm`, Wald test | 0.718 s | **0.558 s** | not taken | not taken |
| `glmm`, score test | 0.896 s | **0.743 s** | 0.996 s | 0.885 s |
| `lmm`, score, approximation on | 0.340 s | **0.191 s** | not taken | not taken |
| `glmm`, approximation on | 0.525 s | **0.379 s** | not taken | not taken |

Against the two programs and the two targets:

- **`lm`.** The target and the number this review took are of different
  thread counts, and they are given apart. "Speed" of `docs/specs/gwas.md`
  names **plink2's 0.10 s on one thread**, where section 2.1 of
  `docs/rust_core.md` measured it; popnei takes 0.322 s on one thread, 3.2
  times that target, against 0.434 s before these changes. Run the way a
  user runs it, at `--threads 18`, plink2 takes 28.7 ms ± 0.3 ms over the
  same 100000 variants from its own `.pgen`, writing the same 100000 rows,
  against popnei's 0.120 s on 18 cores: 4.2 times its wall time, where it
  was 12.7 times before. The target that stands in the spec is the
  one-thread one.
- **`glm`.** The spec states no target. plink2's logistic over the same
  panel takes 206.1 ms ± 3.4 ms on about 15 cores, 3.02 s of processor time;
  popnei's Wald test takes 0.579 s where it took 5.020 s, so it is 2.8 times
  plink2's wall time where it was 24 times, and it is below plink2's
  processor time. The two are not the same fit. Where a variant separates
  the individuals who have the condition from those who have not, its effect
  is infinite rather than large, and popnei answers it with three NaNs;
  plink2 fits that variant again with a term added to the likelihood that
  pulls the effect back towards 0, which is called a Firth penalized fit and
  which popnei does not have. "The logistic model" of the spec records the
  divergence, and it is one variant of this panel.
- **The two mixed models.** The spec's number to reach is GMMAT's 1.6 s
  for both. popnei takes 0.549 s and 0.743 s, and 0.191 s and 0.379 s with
  the GRAMMAR-Gamma approximation, so both are inside that target by 2.2 to
  8.4 times. Nothing of this review ran GMMAT at this size.

**The cost from Python is 10 ms and not worth a change.** No timing of this
module had ever been taken through the boundary. The same continuous study
run from Python, phenotype and covariates as a pandas series and frame and
`stats` as a frame of seven columns with the chromosome, the position and
the id of every variant, takes 0.130 s against the 0.120 s the same study
takes in Rust; the binomial one takes 0.583 s against 0.579 s. The
`python_boundary` reviewer set its own gate at a tenth of the study and four
of its candidates fall below it, so they are filed in section 5 and not
acted on.

### Where the time of the linear model goes now

From the phase clock, at 18 threads, on the panel:

| phase | before the four changes | after |
|---|---|---|
| asking the reader for a block | 0.112 s | 0.025 s, which is the pass waiting |
| the dosages of a block | 0.015 s | 0.016 s |
| testing the variants of a block | 0.076 s | 0.078 s |
| wall time | 0.204 s | 0.120 s |

The reader takes 0.112 s whatever the model and whatever the thread count,
since nothing of it is parallel: it is the lz4 decompression of the
genotypes, 227 MB of output from 77.8 MB on disc, counted from the arrow
footer by the `io_and_syscalls` reviewer. Before the read ahead thread that
was 55 per cent of the linear model's run; now the study waits 0.025 s of it
and hides the rest behind its own work. **The linear model is now bounded by
that reader**: its compute is 0.094 s and its read 0.112 s, so the next gain
for the two unmixed models has to make the vars file cheaper to read, which
is the finding L7 of section 5.

### What is left for the owner to decide

1. **Whether the read ahead reader is taken up by the other passes.** It
   is `with_one_block_ahead` of `crates/popnei/src/block.rs`, a reader over
   a reader that lends its source inside a scoped thread, and the study is
   its only caller. The kinship's own review measured its read at 0.110 s of
   a 0.283 s run, a larger share than the study had, and the principal
   components are the next case. Adopting it is three lines in each pass. It
   costs one more block alive. A block holds the same 5 million genotypes
   wherever the size popnei chooses for the individuals of the source is
   neither held down nor held up, so it is 5000 variants of 1000 individuals
   here and 500 of 10000 there: the block is 10.3 MB on this panel, where
   the resident set of the process grew by 12.8 MB, and 10.0 MB at the
   largest dataset of the objectives. Corrected on 25 September 2026: this
   paragraph and section 6.4 first said 250 variants and 5 MB at 10000
   individuals, half of what `default_num_vars_per_block` gives, which
   `docs/specs/block.md` now states with the two sizes that hold it down and
   up. It also
   changes what "one thread" means in a comparison with `plink2 --threads
   1`, which is why this is the owner's and not the review's.
   Recommendation: yes for the kinship and the principal components, both
   measured before they are kept.
2. **`docs/specs/block.md` owes a spec item.** It says, under "Not in
   this spec", that the read ahead thread comes "with the first calculation
   that consumes blocks". That calculation now exists and the reader is
   written, so the spec sentence is owed either way: as the item, if the
   reader stays, or as the measurement that closed it, if it does not.
3. **Open 2 of `docs/specs/gwas.md` rests on a cost that is not the real
   one.** That item asks whether the linear mixed model's Wald test should
   form the residual exactly per variant instead of refusing a variant whose
   subtraction has cancelled, and the spec and `docs/reports/gwas-linear.md`
   both put the cost of the exact form at "a matrix product for every
   variant, roughly doubling that test". The reviewer showed by algebra that
   it need not be a product at all. Write `p` for the projection matrix,
   individuals by individuals, that every variant of that model is taken
   through; `y` for the trait of the tested individuals, `x` for the dosages
   of one variant, and `beta` for the effect the test gives it. `p` can be
   written as `l l'`, a matrix times its own transpose, which costs one pass
   over the individuals squared times the columns of the design, once per
   study. What the test then needs is `|l' y - beta · l' x|²`, the squared
   length of one vector of as many values as there are individuals: one more
   pass over the individuals for each variant, and no product. The
   block-sized product the test makes today, the block against `p`, becomes
   the block against `l`, the same shape and the same cost. It also takes
   the cancellation out of the denominator, so the two arithmetic backends
   could not disagree on its sign. Nothing of this was measured and it
   changes what both mixed models keep and every column they are verified
   against, so it is a spec question and not a speed one. Recommendation:
   reopen Open 2 with this cost in front of it before deciding, since the
   recommendation it carries, refuse the variant, was chosen against a price
   the exact form does not have to pay.
4. **The GRAMMAR-Gamma approximation is off by default and it halves the
   two mixed models**, 0.549 s to 0.191 s and 0.743 s to 0.379 s. That is
   what it is for and the spec describes it; what is new is the number at
   this size. Nothing needs changing unless the owner wants the default to
   move, which is a question about the numbers and not about the speed.
5. **A study over the 403 MB VCF is now faster than one over the 77.8 MB
   vars file**, 0.103 s against 0.120 s at 18 threads for the linear model,
   because the VCF reader parses its lines on the threads of rayon and the
   vars file reader decompresses lz4 on one. That is a fact about popnei's
   own file format and the owner may want it recorded in
   `docs/specs/io_vars.md`, whose compression was chosen for the size on
   disc and for building without a C compiler. The `io_and_syscalls`
   reviewer measured what the alternative would be: written with no
   compression the reader's lz4 work goes to zero and the file grows 2.9
   times, and arrow-rs reads such a file already.
6. **Whether the branch is merged.** Six commits, every check of the
   coding skill clean at the head, 932 cargo tests and 524 pytest tests
   passing, and every column of every study byte for byte what it was.

## 3. The measurement plan

What this review built, which the next one starts from:

1. **`crates/popnei/benches/gwas.rs`**, the benchmark of the whole study.
   `cargo bench --bench gwas -- <path> --model lm|lmm|glm|glmm [--test
   wald|score] [--covariates n] [--seed n] [--grammar-gamma] [--runs n]`. It
   times `calc_gwas` from building the reader to the result, draws the trait
   and the covariates from a seed so that one seed is one study on every
   machine, and computes the kinship of a mixed model outside the clock.
   `--write-answers <path>` writes the three answer columns of every variant
   at `{:.17e}` from the untimed run, which is how every experiment below
   was held to moving no number.
2. **The phase clock**, the cargo feature `bench-phases` of
   `crates/popnei/Cargo.toml`, off in every build popnei ships. It prints
   how long the pass spent asking its reader for a block, making the
   dosages, and testing the variants. A sampling profile cannot separate the
   first from the third while they share a thread, so this is what decided
   the read ahead thread and what says the read is now hidden.
3. **The profiles** are taken with `/usr/bin/sample <pid> <seconds>` on
   the benchmark with enough runs to fill the window, and read as one thread
   at a time. Build with `--profile profiling`, not `cargo bench`: under
   `[profile.bench]` the line tables leave the linker's folded functions
   unnamed, and 6.2 per cent of the linear mixed model's profile came back
   as `<deduplicated_symbol>`, outranking the model's own self time.
4. **plink2 at this size** is `plink2 --pfile
   /Users/jose/devel/popnei-bench/bigcalled_plink --pheno gwas_pheno.txt
   --pheno-name quant|binom --covar gwas_covar.txt --glm hide-covar --out
   <out> --threads 18`, timed with `hyperfine --warmup 1 --runs 3`. The
   phenotype and the covariate files are beside the panel.

What the next review should measure, in the order that unblocks the findings
of section 5:

5. **The block size, as the cache stand-in.** `Reblock::new(reader,
   None)` of `crates/popnei/src/gwas/pass.rs` takes the size the reader
   gives; with `Some(n)` at 5000, 1024, 256 and 64, at one thread, the time
   per genotype says whether the traffic over the two 40 MB buffers of a
   block is what the tests cost or whether the dependent sums are. It
   decides S1 and H2 of section 5 between them and needs no new code.
6. **The rounds of the Wald fit per variant**, which nothing counts. The
   pass takes 0.579 s and that is rounds times the cost of a round, so no
   change to it can be told from another until the rounds are known. A
   counter behind `bench-internals`, as `variant` and `ld` already re-export
   for their benchmarks.
7. **The scan of the linear algebra crate, as an upper bound.** Build
   with the two scans of `product` removed and time the Wald test: that is
   the most the finding L1 can give, and the promised-operand type it would
   need is worth it only above 0.3 s of the 5.020 s the scan was 13.9 per
   cent of, which is now 0.579 s.
8. **Ten thousand individuals.** `make_big_vcf.py` hard-codes 1000 and
   the two mixed models grow with the square of that, so the ranking above
   is for 1000 individuals and nothing says what it is at the size the
   objectives name.
9. **The wasm build under node**, peak memory and not time: the linear
   mixed model's fit holds three individuals by individuals matrices, 2.4 GB
   at 10000 individuals, in a 32 bit address space of at most 4 GB. The
   deliverable is the largest number of individuals a browser tab can do,
   which `docs/specs/gwas.md` has no cell for.

## 4. The build configuration

All four of the usual settings were closed without an experiment, and the
reason is in the profiles: between 4.6 and 65 per cent of each run is inside
`libBLAS.dylib`, which no link time optimization of popnei reaches.
`[profile.release]` of `Cargo.toml` has `overflow-checks = false` and `debug
= "line-tables-only"`, no `lto`, and 16 codegen units. Link time
optimization and one codegen unit were each measured twice by earlier
reviews with nothing outside 0.004 s
(`docs/reports/perf-kinship-2026-09-23.md`), `target-cpu` moves little on
`aarch64`, and an allocator swap is not indicated: `Vec<f64>::resize` is 24
samples of the 8439 of the logistic score test's main thread. What did
change is which profile a profile is taken on, item 3 of section 3.

## 5. The findings that were not acted on

The four that were acted on are section 6. These are by severity and then by
confidence, in the format of
`.claude/skills/performance-review/finding_format.md`, shortened to what the
next session needs: the site, the evidence, the mechanism, the measurement
that would decide it, and what it would cost.

### Hot-path

**H1. Five library calls per round on a 1000 x 4 design, where two fused
passes would do.** `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:1032-1131`. The Wald
fit of one variant makes about 19 BLAS and LAPACK calls per variant for
about 1.8e5 arithmetic operations, which the `linalg_and_wasm` reviewer put
at 9.4 GFLOP/s against the 467 GFLOP/s the linalg review measured for a
large product; `libBLAS.dylib` is 38.8 per cent of the self time of the Wald
test's one thread, and `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=1` was measured to change
that pass by 0.055 s of 5.020 s, so it is the per call cost and not the
threading. One pass over the design gives the linear predictor, the fitted
chance, the weight, the residual and the lower half of `d' w d` in
registers. Measure: the three shapes added to
`crates/popnei-linalg/benches/ops.rs` as one region of 1.9e6 calls, to price
the path; then the fused round behind a `cfg`. The sums come out in another
order, so the gate is the tolerances of "How it is verified" of the spec
against R's `glm` on both panels and both backends. Cost: a second
implementation of one round of the fit, shared with the null fit.

**H2. The per variant reductions are serial chains of a thousand dependent
additions.** `crates/popnei/src/gwas/linear.rs:341-376` and
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:664-681`. The `hot_loops` and the
`numbers` reviewers agreed on the arithmetic: the linear model makes 3e8
additions in what was 0.194 s, 0.65 ns each, which is one addition's latency
per element and not the memory traffic, since the 2.4 GB those loops stream
would take 0.024 s at 100 GB/s. Splitting each reduction over `k`
accumulators cuts the chain to `1000/k`. Measure the linear model and the
logistic score test after the change, keeping only a fall larger than the
spread of the runs. **The numbers move**: every term of these sums is
non-negative so nothing cancels and the bound improves, but the value itself
moves by up to 2.2e-13 relative at 1000 individuals, and a relative change
in the statistic moves the p-value by about half the statistic times it, 15
times at the panel's strongest hit, against the logistic model's 1e-13 bound
whose worst measured entry is 4.524e-14. Both sides of the threshold of Open
2 move too, so the set of variants that get three NaNs must come out equal
and not close. Cost: a fixed accumulator width that a comment has to
justify, in five loops.

**H3. The block against the projection matrix does twice the arithmetic of a
triangular solve against the covariance's own factor.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/linear_mixed.rs:858` and
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic_mixed.rs:1115`. That product is 59 to 65
per cent of the three mixed model runs and it runs at 440 GFLOP/s against
the library's own 467, so it is at the speed of the machine and what is open
is its operation count. With `L` the Cholesky factor of the covariance, the
matrix that solving against gives what multiplying by `L⁻¹` would, and `Q` a
matrix whose columns have length 1, are at right angles to each other and
span what `L⁻¹` makes of the design, computed once per study, the
denominator `x' p x` of one variant is `‖(I−QQ')L⁻¹x‖²`. That is a lower
triangular solve of the block, half the arithmetic of the product it
replaces, and the logistic mixed model already holds `L`. It is the same
factorization as the answer to Open 2 in section 2, so the two belong
together. Measure: time `solve_triangular` lower at 1000 by 5000 sides
beside the product of that shape in `ops.rs`, and stop unless the solve is
below 0.7 of the product. Cost: the largest of any finding here. Both null
models change what they keep, the approximation and the Wald test read the
projection matrix, the solve overwrites the block so it is copied first, and
every column of both models is re-verified against rrBLUP and GMMAT.

### Likely

**L1. The Wald fit proves the same 1000 x 4 design finite eleven times per
variant.** `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:1035`, `:1071`, `:1111`.
`popnei_linalg::every_value_is_finite`, the scan the linear algebra crate
makes over every value it is given, is 13.9 per cent of the Wald test's one
thread, 8.5 per cent of the logistic score test's and 7.8 per cent of the
linear model's. The count: at three rounds a variant scans 63160 values,
6.3e9 over the pass, of which 44000 per variant are the design and 3000 of
its 4000 values never change in the study. The linalg review closed this
with "leave it" at 2.9 to 3.6 per cent of two other passes and named a
caller it could not reach as what would reopen it; this is that caller.
Measure: item 7 of section 3. Cost: the one that review designed, a type
that carries with a buffer the promise that its values have been checked
already, in fourteen signatures, and the invariant somebody has to keep that
such a buffer is not written between the check and the call.

**L2. One scalar `exp` per individual per round of every Wald fit, through a
lazy-binding stub.** `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:744`, called at
`:1057`. `exp` of the system library is 24.1 per cent of the Wald test's one
thread and the stub that calls it another 2.5 per cent, about 5e8 calls over
the pass. `libm::exp`, which this module already takes `erfc` and `lgamma`
from, is static and one line; `vvexp` of Accelerate, the maths and linear
algebra library of macOS that popnei's native build already calls, computes
the exponential of a whole array in one call, which would also let the three
loops beside it work on several values at a time; it exists on macOS alone,
so it forks the module's hottest arithmetic. Measure: count the calls to the
stub with `objdump -d` on the benchmark binary, which is the same number
every run, then the wall time. The weights move in their last bits and the
fit carries that to about 1e-15 on `beta` and `se`, so the gate is the
discrete decisions of the fit: the effect passing 30, the pivot ratio, the
step that has settled, and `var0006` still the only variant plink2 gave to
Firth.

**L3. The chromosome of every variant crosses into Python as one object per
variant.** `crates/popnei-python/src/gwas.rs:221-227`. Four passes per
variant build it where the core holds codes and a table: a vector of
indices, a vector of Python strings one clone each, a tuple, and pandas
re-encoding that into an Arrow array. Measured on the Python heap by the
reviewer: a million variants of one chromosome peak at 66.0 MB as a tuple
against 1.0 MB as a categorical, 6.8 against 0.1 MB at this panel's
100000. The whole boundary is 10 ms of the 130 ms study, so this is
        filed and not recommended; it becomes worth doing at a million
        variants or if that 10 ms is ever measured to be mostly this. Cost:
        the dtype of `stats["chrom"]` changes, which the spec names the
        column but not the type of, and the same code builds `Block.chrom`.

**L4. The covariates are boxed into Python objects and read cell by cell.**
`python/popnei/gwas.py:704-771`. `to_numpy(dtype=object)` boxes every cell
and a Python call per cell then checks it: measured at 100000 boxed values
and 3.20 MB for 10000 individuals by 10 covariates, which is the shape the
objectives point at, against a float64 view that allocates nothing. Once per
study. Measure the two functions at 1000 by 2 and 10000 by 10 and keep only
above 20 ms. Cost: two paths for one rule, and the invariant that only the
slow one writes the per name messages the spec requires.

**L5. The kinship is cut element by element in TypeScript and copied again
into wasm.** `js/popnei/src/gwas.ts:923-953`. A double loop of n² indexings
and then a copy across the boundary, three n²·8 buffers alive at the call,
where the cut is the matrix itself when every individual is tested. No
TypeScript timing of this module exists. Measure under node at 3000
individuals, gating on the buffer count.

**L6. The id of every variant is cloned into the result.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/pass.rs:409`. `extend_from_slice` over a
`Vec<String>` clones each string where the block is owned by the loop and
dropped next, so 100000 allocations and frees per pass that `Vec::append`
would move. Measure by counting the allocations, which DHAT, the tool that
counts every one a program makes, gives exactly and the same on every run;
`docs/reports/perf-io-2026-09-22.md` has the eight lines that turn it on
behind a cargo feature.

**L7. The vars file reader decompresses on one thread, and that is now what
bounds the two unmixed models.** `crates/popnei/src/io/vars.rs:532`. 227 MB
of lz4 output per pass, 0.112 s, against plink2's whole 28.7 ms run from a
2-bit file of 22.3 MB. Two routes, both outside this module: write the file
uncompressed, which the reader already reads and which makes the file 2.9
times larger, or decompress the columns of a batch on several threads. The
first is measured by writing the panel again and timing the study with the
page cache cold and warm.

### Speculative

**S1. The block is walked five times over two 40 MB buffers.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/linear.rs:292-382`. The `data_layout` reviewer
counted about 400 MB of traffic per block and 8 GB over the panel, neither
buffer fitting any cache, and proposed walking panels of 64 variants that
stay in cache. Its own gate was the linear model falling by more than 0.02
s, and after the change of section 6.2 that loop is on 18 threads and the
gate is harder to meet. The sweep of item 5 of section 3 is what says
whether the traffic is the cost at all, and it costs nothing to run.

**S2. Two vectors per block for what is read once.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/dosages.rs:267-281`. The rows of a block are
collected as a vector of `Result<RowDosages>`, whose error variant is far
wider than the 16 bytes of the row, and the block then walks it for one
number and one flag per variant. Gate on the size of the element and on one
allocation per block fewer.

**S3. Three `lgamma` per variant whose arguments are fixed for the study.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/distributions.rs:114-116`. `a` is half the degrees
of freedom and `b` is 0.5 for the whole study, and their three terms are one
constant. The whole file is 4 per cent of the linear model, so 0.7 per cent
is the ceiling; bit identical if the constant keeps the order of the three
calls. Worth doing only alongside another change in that file.

**S4. Each batch's bytes are zeroed before being read over.**
`crates/popnei/src/io/vars.rs:2913-2921`. 77.8 MB memset per pass that
`read_exact` then overwrites; `__bzero` is 0.4 per cent of the linear
model's thread and is shared with other callers. Gate on bytes memset.

**S5. The second pass of the GRAMMAR-Gamma approximation decompresses a
batch that the pass after it decompresses again.**
`crates/popnei/src/gwas/pass.rs:333-370`. 5.0 per cent more lz4 output on a
run with the approximation, plus a second open and footer parse. Taking it
out changes the spec: the factor would come from the first block of the pass
that tests the variants, before it tests them, so the second reader
`calc_gwas` is given, the three errors about it and the argument both
bindings pass for it all go with it. The owner's call.

### Notes

- **The weighting of the design has a trip count the study carries.**
  `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:1061-1070`. The inner loop runs
  `num_coefs` times, 4 on this panel but a runtime value, 4.5e8 times over
  the pass. `the_codes_of_the_genotypes` of `crates/popnei/src/variant.rs`
  dispatches on a constant ploidy for exactly this reason. `cargo asm`
  refutes it at no cost if the listing is already unrolled.
- **`the_called_dosages` mixes accumulators of three widths in one pass.**
  `crates/popnei/src/gwas/dosages.rs:211`. Two `u64` and two `u8` in one
  body, so the vector factor follows the widest. All of the wall time on
  wasm, diluted by 18 threads here. Its branchless form cannot be justified
  by this panel, where every genotype is called and the predicate is
  perfectly predicted.
- **The three columns the pass carries cost 0.89 per cent of the
  decompression.** The `io_and_syscalls` reviewer counted the bytes: 2.0 MB
  of the chromosome, the position and the id against 225.0 MB of genotypes,
  and an unprojected column only advances an iterator. Asking for every
  field would add 0.29 per cent. There is nothing here.
- **`Reblock` copies nothing on this panel**, since the vars file's
  batches are already the size popnei chooses for 1000 individuals. With a
  filter in front, or a file written at another batch size, every row is
  copied once, which is what that reader is for.

## 6. What the experiments showed

Each experiment was delegated with a fresh context, took its own baseline
before its change, ran the correctness checks of the coding skill before
looking at any timing, and gated on the three answer columns of all 100000
variants being byte for byte what they were. Every one of them passed that
gate at one thread and at eighteen.

### 6.1 The Wald fit of a variant on the threads of rayon: applied at `8220022`

5.010 s to 0.679 s, 7.4 times, on 100000 variants x 1000 individuals at 18
cores. `wald_test_the_block` fitted one logistic regression per variant in a
loop on the thread of the pass, and 16706 of the 16781 samples of that
pass's profile were on that one thread while 17 cores idled. The fits are
independent, so each thread of the pool now takes a set of the fit's buffers
of its own and works through the variants it is handed, which is what the
dosages of a block already did; the answers are collected in the order of
the block and the first error in that order too, so neither depends on how
many threads there are.

What it cost: `Clone` on the fit, 94 KB per clone and one per rayon split
per block, not per variant; two vectors of 5000 per block; a path that fits
one variant after another under `target_family = "wasm"`, where there are no
threads; and a test that compares the two ways on pools of one and four
threads, seen to fail with the answers reversed on purpose.

Two things the finding did not say. The scaling is near ideal and there is
nothing left to win by threading this further: 0.4 s of serial work plus 4.6
s over 18 cores predicts 0.66 s against the 0.679 s measured. And on the
faer backend, which is what a browser runs and what `--no-default-features`
builds, `the_threads()` asks for the global rayon pool on every call, so the
fits of 18 workers each ask faer for that pool for a 1000 x 4 product;
nothing broke and 932 tests pass, but nothing has timed the Wald test on
faer at this size. Under wasm the serial path runs and faer is sequential,
so a tab is untouched.

### 6.2 The per variant loops of the two unmixed tests on rayon:
applied at `ee822f0`

The linear model 0.343 s to 0.206 s, 1.67 times; the logistic score test
0.276 s to 0.194 s, 1.42 times, at 18 cores. On one thread neither moves:
two clean pairs of measurements straddle zero against a spread of 0.02 s.
Every reduction in those loops lives inside one variant, which is why the
columns are equal and not merely close at any number of threads.

What it cost: 526 lines added over three files. Each test gained a type that
carries what a row is read with, a method that answers one row, a rayon
version, a serial twin for wasm, and a test comparing the two. The three
pushes per variant became one value written by its index, so the variant
that has no answer carries its three NaNs where it stands instead of being
skipped.

### 6.3 The squared length of a variant taken where its dosages are
written: applied at `4b57673`

The linear mixed model 0.699 s to 0.651 s and the logistic mixed one 0.878 s
to 0.837 s at 18 cores, 0.040 to 0.048 s in each of three interleaved rounds
of measurement; the linear model does not move. Three reviewers found the
same site from three directions: the sum of the squares of a variant's
dosages was reduced again in the test, a second read of the block's 40 MB,
and all it is used for is the scale of the threshold that refuses a variant
with nothing left. It is now accumulated where the row is written, on the
rayon threads with the row in cache, in the same order, so the value is bit
identical on the panel with every genotype called and on the one with 3 in
100 missing.

**The gain is not where the finding's plan put it.** `data_layout` gated on
the linear model falling by more than 0.01 s, and it falls by nothing: the
change of 6.2 had already put that model's loop on 18 threads, so its second
read of the dosages was already spread and already in cache. The two mixed
models still test the variants of a block one after another, and that is
where the 0.04 s came from.

One limit of the identity check, worth knowing before the next such change:
both panels answer all 100000 variants, so no variant on either takes the
three NaNs and the panel comparison exercises the threshold's arithmetic and
never its refusal. What covers the refusal is the unit tests, which put a
variant with no answer at every fifth row.

### 6.4 The study reads one block ahead of the block in hand:
applied at `a26b2dd` and `f5c8033`

The linear model 0.198 s to 0.120 s, the logistic score test 0.182 s to
0.119 s, the linear mixed model 0.643 s to 0.548 s and the logistic Wald
test 0.680 s to 0.580 s at 18 cores; 1.30 to 1.31 times on one thread for
the two unmixed models, and nothing for the Wald test on one thread, where
the reading thread costs about what the overlap saves.

This is the finding that `docs/architecture.md` has asked for since before
any code, that `docs/reports/perf-io-2026-09-22.md` and
`docs/reports/perf-kinship-2026-09-23.md` each left open, and that no pass
of popnei had. Both of those reports said to instrument before building it,
and that is what the phase clock is: the read came out at 0.112 s of a 0.204
s run against a gate of 0.04 s, so the finding held by 2.8 times.

It is `with_one_block_ahead` of `crates/popnei/src/block.rs`, not of the
gwas module, because the kinship review said it belongs there and every pass
can then take it. One reading thread, one rendezvous channel of capacity
zero so that exactly one block is ever built and not given, and the source
lent inside a `std::thread::scope` so that whoever built the chain of
filters still reads its counts when the pass ends. How each promise is kept:
the error travels as a message of its own and nothing follows it, which the
test of a VCF whose 251st variant has the wrong ploidy asserts at two blocks
then the error then nothing; a change to which fields a consumer asks its
reader to fill crosses on a channel of its own and reaches the source before
its next block, so it takes effect one block later than it would on one
thread, which is what section 1 of `docs/architecture.md` already promises;
under wasm there is no thread, no channel and no handle; and the thread
cannot leak on an early return because the only receiver is a local of the
scope, so the blocked send fails and the thread ends, which the experiment
proved by trying to break it rather than by a guard.

What it cost: 146 lines of code, 160 of comment and 97 of test in
`block.rs`, three private types and one public function, nothing in the
error enum so neither binding changed, and one more block alive: the block
is 10.3 MB on this panel and the resident set of the process grew by 12.8
MB; at 10000 individuals, where a block of the same 5 million genotypes
holds 500 variants, it is 10.0 MB again, which section 2 corrects. No test fails before this change and passes
after it, because nothing a caller sees is different; the four new tests are
of the reader itself and each was seen to fail with the code broken on
purpose.

### 6.5 What was refuted, and what it saved

- **Accelerate's threads are not what the tiny products of the Wald fit
  cost.** The hypothesis was that 38.8 per cent of that pass in
  `libBLAS.dylib`, on matrices of 1000 x 4 and 4 x 4 called 1.9 million
  times, was the library's own threading. `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=1` gave
  4.965 s against 5.020 s with every thread. What is left is the per call
  cost, which is H1.
- **The Python boundary is 10 ms of a 130 ms study** and 4 ms of a 583 ms
  one, so the four candidates of the `python_boundary` reviewer fall below
  the gate that reviewer set for itself. They are L3, L4 and L5.
- **The aggregation of a profile across threads is not wall clock time.**
  The briefing this review gave its reviewers ranked the samples of every
  thread together, which reads work on 18 rayon workers as 18 times its
  share of the wall time: the dosages of a block came out at 18.0 per cent
  where the main thread's own unlisted remainder puts them near 1.6. The
  `methodology` reviewer caught it and no finding rested on it, because
  every headline share above is of one thread. The rankings in this report
  are per thread and say which thread.
- **The linear model's block size and its cache traffic are still
  unmeasured**, and the sweep that would settle S1 and H2 between them costs
  nothing but the runs. It was not run because the four experiments above
  filled the machine's quiet hours.

## 7. Seen outside the scope

- **`dpotrf` is missing from the table of routines measured to answer the
  same while other threads are inside Accelerate.**
  `crates/popnei-linalg/src/blas.rs:414`, and the table is "Calling the
  crate from two threads" of `docs/specs/linalg.md`. `dpotrf` is the routine
  of LAPACK that factors a matrix into a triangular one times its own
  transpose, and `cholesky_lower`, the crate's name for it, calls it on
  every fit of every variant of the Wald test, which now runs on 18 threads,
  so the gap in what was verified is load bearing where it was not before.
  No wrong result is known.
- **`crates/popnei-linalg` has no matrix by vector routine.** Every
  product with one column goes to `dgemm` with one row, `blas.rs:97` and
  `:182`, including two of the three products of every round of every Wald
  fit and two block-sized ones that read 40 MB that way.
- **The fast lz4 decoder is in use by accident.** `arrow-ipc` takes
  `lz4_flex` with its default features off, so `safe-decode` and
  `checked-decode` are off, and it is the only crate in `Cargo.lock` that
  takes it. A future dependency with the defaults turns both on for the vars
  reader and nothing would say so; `cargo tree -f "{p} {f}"` is the guard.
- **Two doc comments say more than they should.**
  `crates/popnei/src/gwas/dosages.rs:236` says each thread keeps the buffers
  of one row, where rayon's `map_init` gives one per job and not one per
  thread; and `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:80-87` says a pass asks
  the machine for nothing per variant, while the pass it cites made 9447
  allocations for 2000 variants in the linear algebra crate under it.
  Neither is a wrong result and both are for a code review.

## 8. What the code already does well

- **Every buffer of the per variant logistic fit is made once per study**
  and none per variant, `crates/popnei/src/gwas/logistic.rs:877-890`. The
  `allocations` reviewer found no hot-path allocation anywhere in the
  module: no `format!`, no `to_string`, no `Box::new` on any per variant or
  per block path, and the 39 allocations per variant its own doc comment
  names are the linear algebra crate's on the faer backend, which no timing
  here runs.
- **The block is the unit of every product but the per variant fit**, ten
  sites listed by the `linalg_and_wasm` reviewer, and the one product that
  dominates the mixed models runs at 440 GFLOP/s against the 467 the
  library's own benchmark reaches. There is nothing to win there by writing
  it differently.
- **The threshold that refuses a variant with nothing left is written once
  for all four models**, `crates/popnei/src/gwas/mod.rs:134`, hoisted out of
  every per variant loop, one multiply and one compare. The two quantities
  the spec measured and chose to form rather than subtract, the residual sum
  of squares of the linear model and the denominator of the logistic score
  test, are still formed, and three reviewers checked that no change
  proposed here undoes them.
